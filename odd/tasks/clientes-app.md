## Specs

### C1 — Registration: each app says which role it is creating

- User wording (L2): “Cada app indica su rol (Recomendado)”
- `POST /auth/otp/verify` accepts an OPTIONAL app indication, `registrationRole`, and it applies ONLY when that verification creates the account.
- The allowed values are exactly `"client"` and `"tendero"`. Every other value — including the stored-but-frozen roles `admin`, `marca` and `delivery` — is rejected.
- Absent means today's default, which the backend process configures (`defaultRegistrationRole: "tendero"` in `apps/Tenderos/backend/src/app.ts`).
- An EXISTING account always keeps its stored role. The indication can never change it, which is what makes this not a privilege path: role selection exists at account creation and nowhere else.
- Acceptance: a verification of an unknown email with `registrationRole: "client"` creates a user whose `role` is `client`; with `registrationRole: "tendero"`, and with the field absent, it creates a `tendero`; `registrationRole: "admin"`, `"marca"`, `"delivery"` or any unknown string answers `400 {"error":"Invalid request payload.", "details": …}` and creates NO user; a verification of an existing `tendero` sent with `registrationRole: "client"` answers `200` with `role: "tendero"` and the stored `role_id` is unchanged; a verification that carries no `registrationRole` returns the same body shape and the same `role` it returns today.
- Acceptance (the shared-email consequence, documented rather than fixed): a customer who signs in with an email that already registered as a `tendero` KEEPS the `tendero` role and therefore answers `403 {"error":"You do not have access to this resource."}` on every `/api/v1/clientes/*` route. One email is one account and one role in this MVP; `docs/api-clientes.md` states it so the customer app can word the dead end instead of looping.

### C2 — A customer needs a name before claiming

- User wording (L2): “Sí, en el registro (Recomendado)”
- A new `client` starts in a state that REQUIRES a name: `registrationStatus` is `profile_required` and `fullName` is `""`, exactly as today.
- Saving a valid name through `PATCH /auth/me` COMPLETES a client's registration: `profile_required → completed`. There is no store step for a customer, so `store_required` is not a state a `client` can be in.
- The tendero's transitions are untouched: `profile_required → store_required` through `PATCH /auth/me`, and `store_required → completed` through the tendero-only `POST /auth/registration/complete`.
- `POST /api/v1/clientes/coupons/:couponId/claims` rejects a customer whose stored name has no non-whitespace character, with `409 {"error":"Add your name before claiming a coupon."}`, and leaves stock and claim counters unchanged.
- Acceptance: a `client` who sends one valid `fullName` reads `registrationStatus: "completed"` and stays `completed` on a second write; a `tendero` in `profile_required` still reads `store_required` after the same call and a `completed` tendero still stays `completed`; a nameless `client` claiming a claimable coupon answers the 409 above with the coupon's `claimed_count` and the stored claim count unchanged, and the same customer claims successfully (`201`) right after saving a name.
- Acceptance (why the gate is the NAME and not `registrationStatus`): the tendero's counter confirms the person in front of them against `customerName`, which the scanner response derives from `users.full_name` (`packages/backend-core/src/coupons/customer-name.ts`). A status value the customer app could reach some other way would not put a name on that screen; the stored name is the fact that does. `PATCH /auth/me` is NOT tightened to reject a whitespace-only name, because that route is the tendero's too and its validation is frozen by the hard constraint — the claim gate tests the same emptiness instead.

### C3 — Discovery without location permission

- User wording (L2): “Seguidas + pedir permiso (Recomendado)”
- `GET /api/v1/clientes/coupons/feed` accepts NO coordinates at all. With neither `lat` nor `lng` it answers the claimable coupons of the customer's FOLLOWED stores only, each item carrying `distanceMeters: null`, `distanceKm: null` and `followed: true`.
- Exactly one of `lat`/`lng` is a `400`: half a position is not a position. The pair stays all-or-nothing.
- The coordinate grammar of L13 is unchanged and still applies when the pair IS sent: a blank, a space, a lone sign, a hex or an exponent spelling is a `400` before anything is converted.
- `GET /api/v1/clientes/stores/nearby` still REQUIRES both coordinates. It is the map, and a map without a position has nothing to draw.
- Acceptance: the feed with no query string answers `200` with only followed stores' claimable coupons, every item `followed: true` and both distance fields `null`, and a nearby non-followed store's coupon is absent; `?lat=4.711` alone and `?lng=-74.0721` alone each answer `400`; `?lat=&lng=` and `?lat=1e1&lng=-74.0721` still answer `400`; the full pair answers exactly what it answers today; `stores/nearby` with no coordinates still answers `400`.

### C4 — Releasing a claim

- User wording (L2): “Sí, puede liberarlo (Recomendado)”
- `POST /api/v1/clientes/claims/:claimId/release` releases one of the customer's OWN claims: `requireRole(["client"])`, own claims only, no body, `200 { "data": CustomerClaim }` with `status: "released"` and `qrToken: null`.
- In ONE transaction, with a lock on the claim row: an unredeemed, unexpired, unreleased claim is marked released and the reserved unit is returned to stock EXACTLY once (`coupons.claimed_count` decremented by one).
- The released claim's QR token stops working everywhere it could still be used: `POST /api/v1/tenderos/coupon-redemptions/preview`, `POST /api/v1/tenderos/coupon-redemptions` and `POST /api/v1/clientes/claims/:claimId/qr` all answer `409 {"error":"This claim was released and is no longer valid."}`.
- Releasing twice is a `409 {"error":"This claim was already released."}` and NEVER a second decrement. A redeemed claim answers `409 {"error":"This claim has already been redeemed."}` and an expired one `409 {"error":"This coupon is no longer valid."}` — the wording the QR re-issue already uses for the same two states.
- A claim that does not exist is `404 {"error":"Claim not found."}` and another customer's claim is `403 {"error":"This claim belongs to another customer."}`, the readings T5 established.
- A PAUSED coupon and a DEACTIVATED store are deliberately not checked, the same way the redemption does not check them (S6, L6, L11): both block NEW claims only, and refusing a release would trap the customer in a coupon they cannot use and cannot give back. The unit returns to a counter nobody can claim from until the coupon is active again, which is the correct state.
- A release racing a redemption: exactly one wins. Both paths take the same row lock (`for update of coupon_claims`) and both carry their precondition in the WHERE of their own UPDATE, so the loser is rejected rather than overwriting the winner.
- `UNIQUE(coupon_id, customer_user_id)` STAYS, so the claim row survives as data and a re-claim of the same coupon is `409 {"error":"You already released this coupon and cannot claim it again."}`.
- ASSUMPTION the user may still correct: a customer who released a coupon CANNOT claim it again. S6 of the cuponera feature says “Una vez por cliente”, and the release does not undo the fact that this customer took it once. If the user wants a release to restore the right to claim, the unique index has to go and the answer becomes a second claim rather than a 409.
- Schema, additive only: `coupon_claims.released_on` is a NULLABLE `date` column plus the new CHECK `coupon_claims_release_chk` (`released_on is null or status <> 'redeemed'`). The generated migration issues zero `DROP` and no `ALTER` that removes anything; `coupon_claims_status_chk` keeps its two stored values (`claimed`, `redeemed`) and `coupons_claimed_within_stock_chk` keeps holding, because the decrement is guarded by `claimed_count > 0`.
- `released` is therefore DERIVED in the response and not a stored status, exactly as `expired` is: the row stays `claimed` and carries a `released_on` date. The wallet SHOWS it (`CustomerClaimStatus` gains `"released"`) rather than hiding the claim, because a disappearing coupon reads as a bug to the person who released it; hiding it would also make the re-claim 409 unexplainable.
- Tendero metrics stay correct: a released claim does NOT count in `claimedCount`, never appears in `redeemedCount` or in the redemption history, and `remainingStock` — derived from the `claimed_count` reservation counter — goes back up by one. The funnel keeps `redeemedCount <= claimedCount` by construction.
- Acceptance: release of a `claimed` claim answers `200` with `status: "released"`, the stored `released_on` set, `claimed_count` one lower and the claim row still present; the wallet then reads `status: "released"` with `qrToken: null`; the three QR paths answer the released message; a second release answers the already-released message with `claimed_count` unchanged; two parallel releases of one claim produce exactly one `200`, one `409` and one decrement; a release racing a redemption produces exactly one winner and the loser's state is never half-applied; a redeemed claim and an expired claim cannot be released; re-claiming the released coupon answers the re-claim 409 and leaves `claimed_count` unchanged; the store's metrics after a release show the claim gone from `claimedCount`, absent from the history, and `remainingStock` restored.
- Known consequence, documented not fixed: the feed's `claimed` flag stays `true` for a released coupon, because the claim row is still there and the customer cannot take it again. The card must read that flag as “already taken by me”, not as “in your wallet”.

### C5 — Deep links are out of the MVP

- User wording (L2): “No en el MVP (Recomendado)”
- No shared-coupon link, no universal/app link, no single-coupon customer endpoint is added by this feature. A coupon stays reachable only through the feed or the wallet, which is what `docs/api-clientes.md` already records as a gap.
- Acceptance: no route, scheme, contract or configuration for a link-shared coupon exists after this feature; the gap stays listed as an open item instead of being silently closed.
